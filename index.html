Before your window survey: a Livingston homeowner's checklist

Published for Windows and Doors Livingston. This is a business guide, not an independent review.

A useful window survey starts before anyone measures the first opening. Make a short list of the rooms you want assessed, the doors that are difficult to use, and the problems you have noticed. A draught, misting between panes or a damaged frame is a reason to ask for an assessment, not a diagnosis you need to make yourself.

Windows and Doors Livingston describes a survey-first approach on its website. The visit checks openings, cills, reveals, existing frames, ventilation and how each room is used. A written quote follows once the surveyor has checked what the property needs.

Prepare a room-by-room list

Rather than asking for the same specification everywhere, explain what matters in each room. Is the priority comfort, easier operation, a matching finish or reducing outside noise? Ask the surveyor what can stay, what should change and why. The business offers double glazing, UPVC windows and triple glazing, with suitability confirmed at the survey.

Include the doors in the conversation

If you are considering a new entrance or back door, mention it when arranging the visit. The business says UPVC and composite doors can be discussed in the same survey when that suits the property. Check the opening style, finish and hardware together instead of treating them as unrelated choices.

Read the quote as a scope of work

Ask which openings are included, what specification has been agreed, and what finishing and disposal are covered. Keep any questions with the written quote so you can compare the proposed work rather than a headline price alone. Check any property restrictions before committing to a replacement.

Plan access before installation

Tell the business about shared access, parking limits and anything inside the home that could affect the work. Confirm appointments before visiting the business address.
